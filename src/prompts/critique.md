## Now be your own toughest judge (call `submit_critique`)
Score the draft 1-10 on each criterion, honestly; a 10 means nothing could be better. Then list the concrete fixes, most important first, and return the complete revised plan with every fix applied (all scenes and all claims, not a diff).

- **accuracy**: every statement matches the evidence and obeys its caveats; distances and "near" are honest; dates and ride order are honest; no living people; no borders.
- **story_craft**: a clear arc (hook -> ride through time -> "they kept coming" -> personal last stop); vivid, specific pictures; a good rhythm of map and illustration scenes; the map does real work.
- **warmth**: one person talking, with affection and a little humour; the city's voice, not a guidebook.
- **pacing**: one idea per scene; no lists of dates (the year card shows the era: at most one date and one other number per scene); one sensory detail per stop; sentences easy to speak and to read as subtitles; no parentheses, no hedges, no "it" or "the Court" before the thing is named.
- **hook**: the first sentence (at most 10 words) makes a stranger stay: a concrete picture or promise, not an abstraction.
- **ending**: the "me and this city" ending lands: {{personal_subject}} and the city; the last personal scene ends on its reveal after a pause; it makes viewers think of their own city.
- **positivity**: the city as a place of arrivals that keeps rebuilding itself; hard history told with care, from all sides.
- **length**: {{min_words}}-{{max_words}} words, {{min_scenes}}-{{max_scenes}} scenes, at most {{max_images}} image scenes, hook within 5 seconds.

Keep everything that already works. Every factual statement in the revised narration must appear in claims with source ids from the evidence.
